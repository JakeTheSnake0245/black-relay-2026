Security controls

  • Starts locked. After every power-on, reboot or Lock: no internet, services or tailnet until the USB key or an admin + vault passphrase unlocks it. The tailnet identity, host secrets and service data sit in an encrypted vault.
  • Fail closed. Site traffic can only leave through the tunnel; if it is down, traffic is dropped, never sent out a raw modem.
  • Leak guard. Only the encrypted lanes to the hub (TLS 443, each pinned to its modem), DHCP and modem admin pages (plus a recognised Starlink dish's own app ports) may use a modem.
  • Hostile modems can't steer traffic. Routes a modem pushes are ignored, leases overlapping the site are refused, and site addresses always route via the router.
  • Hub inside existing ports. No new public ports on BR2026: lanes ride HTTPS 443 behind Caddy; a secret path plus the tunnel's own keys authenticate the site.
  • Hub egress is public-only. Site traffic can't reach the hub's internal networks or private ranges; the hub's own address only on 80/443.
  • No rogue DHCP on any port. DHCP replies and IPv6 router adverts from a port only reach the modem side; nothing plugged in can take over the site LAN's addressing.
  • Remote control needs Pocket ID. The controller's remote listener accepts only the hub, and every person signs in (admin or view-only by group).
  • Least privilege. Proxmox token: view + power only; multipath changes through fixed commands; the three core containers can't be stopped from the UI.
  • No side doors. SSH is key-only; no tailnet forwarders; the on-site password works on the site LAN only (break-glass).
  • Self-healing. Moved or pulled cables, late-booting modems, DHCP changes, stuck lanes and a wedged tunnel recover on their own; up about 2 min after power-on (locked).
